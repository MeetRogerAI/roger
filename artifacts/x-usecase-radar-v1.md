## What the data says (editorial angles)
- **Ordinary-people adoption is real.** "My parents / my wife (not techy) now use Muse" posts show up every week. This is the hook for readers who aren't on X.
- **Owner-side stories are rare. Agents calling businesses are not.** Most "customer service" posts are consumers whose agents phone, email or order from businesses. For BIA members the strongest recurring theme is **"Your next customer is an agent."** Examples:
  - eevblog's first agent-placed order
  - agents calling 82 pool companies for quotes
  - the Meta + Sierra Personal Agent Protocol
- **"Hire your first AI employee" is the frame people respond to.** Elon's post on it drew 42K likes and 62M views. It's a usable series name for the business slots.
- **Keep the cautionary tales.** They build trust with skeptical owners:
  - a Muse running Facebook Marketplace accepted a lowball offer and sent a buyer to the house
  - a failed flight booking still showed a $1,094.99 pending charge
  - a shared-memory slip cost $4,000

## Search method: lessons learned
1. **The agent-name anchor works, but "Muse" is noisy.** It matches K-pop, fashion and sports fan accounts with "Muse" in the handle (e.g. MarinerMuse). Fixes:
   - Prefer "Meta Muse", "my Muse" or "@Muse".
   - Add `-kpop -fashion`.
   - Exclude stock chatter with `-$META -$AMZN -$BKNG`.
2. **Launch weeks swamp the business categories.** 70–90% of Bookkeeping, Shop ops and Teams posts were launch news or stock commentary. Expect qualifying rates of 5–20% there, versus 15–30% for consumer categories.
3. **Long combined queries fail.** Merging three business categories into one query returned an error with 0 posts. Keep one category per query.
4. **Browser scraping has a hard budget.** One logged-in account gets about 7 full searches per ~2 hours of rest. After ~15 back-to-back searches X shows "Something went wrong" or stops paginating at ~20 posts. Measuring 30 category-weeks took most of a day, and three category-weeks were still lost to throttling.
5. **Two ways around X's limits work (found Oct 8):**
   - **Click Retry.** When X shows "Something went wrong" mid-scroll, clicking its Retry button resumes pagination with the posts already collected kept.
   - **Slide the time window.** When X silently stops loading (no error, no Retry button), start a new search whose end time is the oldest post collected so far. Money Wk 2 took 11 windows of 4–50 posts each to cover the full week (270 posts).
   Both cost one search per window, so the ~7-searches-per-2-hours budget still applies. The API recommendation stands.
6. **@Musecases (Muse) and @GrokBotRadar (Grok) reliably surface story-shaped posts.** Use them as tip sources, but credit the original poster. Peter's lane is what they don't cover: all agents, local main-street businesses, and how to set it up.

## Recommendation: switch weekly counts to the X API
- **Pricing** (third-party guides, Sept 2026): X is pay-per-use.
  - Post reads cost about $0.005 each.
  - The 7-day **counts** endpoint costs about $0.01 per request, so 15 categories is about $0.15 per week.
  - Pulling the top ~100 posts for each of the 5 picks costs about $2.50 per week.
  - Realistic total: **~$10–30/month**.
- **Coverage:** recent search only goes back 7 days, so it has to run weekly. It gives exact counts instead of extrapolated ones, with no throttling.
- **Setup:** Peter has to sign up on the X developer portal and add a payment method himself. Then give the key to Claude for the weekly run.

## Weekly workflow (from next week)
1. **Monday, ~1 hour:** get counts for all 15 queries (API, or browser for the 5 picks only). Pull the top posts by likes for the 5 picks plus the rotating slot.
2. **Pick per category:** take the best qualifying story, with at least 2 backups. Prefer real owners and ordinary people over builders.
3. **Draft:**
   - 5–6 X posts: story + what it means + how to try it
   - one BIA newsletter section: a "this week on Main Street" lead, then the 5 one-paragraph stories
4. **Monthly:** re-rank all 15 categories and swap a weekly slot if a category stays above another for 4 weeks.
