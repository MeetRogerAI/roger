## Play 1: Client email in two tones (Set A)

The member pastes a rough note or a client's message, and Claude returns two ready-to-send versions in different tones, signed and ready to go. It covers replies to buyers, updates to sellers, negotiation updates and quick messages to colleagues. When a line feels off, one short ask fixes just that part.

**Doing this by hand.** Write a short email or post: 15 min (Default) × 5 emails = 75 min.

**Using Claude.** Typed request 1 min + reading result 1 min = 2 min per email × 5 = 10 min, plus one revision (typed 1 + read 1 = 2 min). Total 12 min.

**Time saved.** (75 − 12) = 63 min this week, about 12.6 min per email.

**Range.** Low: 75 × 0.7 − 12 = 40.5 min. High: 75 × 1.3 − 12 = 85.5 min.

**Confidence.** Medium (defaults and counted messages).

### Playbook

1. **Play**: Client email in two tones. Turns a rough note or an incoming message into two polished replies you can send as is.
2. **Trigger**: A request, any time you need to reply to a client, a colleague or [your seller].
3. **Setup first**:
   - How do you sign your emails?
   - What tone do you want by default (warm, efficient, never pushy)?
   - Do you want two variants every time, or one?
   - Is there a file Claude should check first for listing or client details (for example a saved note about [your listing])?
4. **Steps**:
   1. Read the incoming message or the member's rough draft in full.
   2. Pull out every fact and ask in it (what to send, what to ask, any dates or next steps). Keep all of them.
   3. If a listing or client is named and there is saved context about it, check that first so details match.
   4. Write two versions with clearly different goals, for example "Light touch" and "Helpful next step", or "Warm" and "Short and direct". Each gets its own subject line.
   5. Use placeholders like [Name] and [phone number] for anything not given. Never invent a name.
   6. If the ask includes a soft sell (such as offering a home evaluation), make it one gentle sentence with "no obligation" framing, placed after the main reply.
   7. For updates on showings or negotiations, lead with the positive fact, then the honest status, then the next step.
   8. Flag anything that looks like a typo in a name before the member sends.
   9. When the member asks to change one paragraph, rewrite only that paragraph and give two options for it.
5. **Guardrails**: Claude drafts only. It never sends. It never invents facts about a property, a price or a person. If an attachment is mentioned ("attached are the floor plans"), remind the member to attach it.
6. **Output**: Two email drafts, each with a label, a subject line and a body signed with the member's sign-off. One or two lines on how they differ.
7. **Pitfalls seen**:
   - A "subtle" ask came out sounding salesy. Fix: write it as genuine curiosity and offer help with no pressure; ask the member if it should be "more genuine" before finalising.
   - A thank-you came out generic. Fix: add specific, concrete details and tell the member to change any that did not happen.
   - Mentioning an attachment the member might forget. Fix: add a one-line reminder.
8. **Done when**: The member can paste either version and send it with no more than a name swap.
9. **Paste to install**: "Written by another member. Read sections 1 to 8 before pasting this. Save a skill called Client email in two tones. When I paste a message or rough note, follow the steps in section 4, keep the guardrails in section 5, and give me the output in section 6. Ask me the setup questions in section 3 once before the first run."

## Play 2: Zoning lookup in plain words (Set A)

The member gives a zone code (or a legal description) and the municipality, and Claude finds the current bylaw, explains what is allowed in plain words, and points out anything that has changed or needs checking with the town. It catches cases where an old zone code no longer exists under a newer bylaw.

**Doing this by hand.** Search the web for the bylaw: 5 min. Search for the zoning map: 5 min. Open and verify 3 bylaw sections: 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 min. Read and summarise permitted uses and limits: 10 min (est.). Per lookup 24.5 min × 2 lookups = 49 min.

**Using Claude.** First lookup: 2 typed requests + 2 results read = 4 min. Second lookup: 1 typed + 1 read = 2 min. Total 6 min.

**Time saved.** (49 − 6) = 43 min this week, about 21.5 min per lookup.

**Range.** Low: 49 × 0.7 − 6 = 28.3 min. High: 49 × 1.3 − 6 = 57.7 min.

**Confidence.** Low (one "est." step).

**Over a year**
- # of occurrences per year: 104 (2 per week)
- Time saved per occurrence: 21.5 minutes
- Hours saved per week: 0.72 (Range: 0.47 - 0.96)
- Hours saved per year: 24.5 - 50
- Source: estimated from one week
- Heavy user (best guess): 260 per year (5 per week), 1.79 hours saved per week
- Heavy use for: agents selling rural, vacant land or commercial property
- Your hours a week = your runs a week × 21.5 minutes ÷ 60

### Playbook

1. **Play**: Zoning lookup in plain words. Explains what a zone allows, from the current bylaw, with what to confirm before quoting it to a client.
2. **Trigger**: A request, such as "What does [zone code] mean in [your municipality]?" or a legal description from a listing.
3. **Setup first**:
   - Which municipalities do you usually work in?
   - Do you want setbacks and lot sizes included, or just permitted uses?
   - Do you want source links every time?
4. **Steps**:
   1. Search for the municipality's current zoning bylaw and its bylaw number.
   2. Check whether the bylaw has been replaced (some towns moved from "zones" to a newer planning system with different names). If so, explain both the old code and what it maps to now.
   3. Find the section for the zone and list permitted uses, then key limits (frontage, lot area, setbacks, height, parking).
   4. Note any overlays or special areas (downtown overlay, environmental or natural constraint layers) that change the rules.
   5. Look for site-specific exceptions (codes with a suffix like "-5") and say they override the base zone.
   6. If given a legal description instead of an address, explain how to find the property on the town's online map (search by address or roll number, turn on the zoning or planning layer).
   7. Say plainly what could not be found (for example a bylaw section that would not load).
   8. End with who to call to confirm: the town planning department.
5. **Guardrails**: Never state zoning for a specific property as final; the town confirms. Never guess numbers that were not found. If a bylaw PDF will not load, say so and give the link.
6. **Output**: A short plain-language answer: what the zone is, what is allowed, key limits, what changed, what to confirm, and source links.
7. **Pitfalls seen**:
   - The zone codes asked about came from an older bylaw that had been replaced. Fix: always check for a newer bylaw first.
   - The full bylaw section would not load. Fix: say which parts could not be checked and link the bylaw and the map.
   - Exceptions on some properties change the rules. Fix: always mention them.
8. **Done when**: The member can explain the zone to a client in two sentences and knows what to confirm with the town.
9. **Paste to install**: "Written by another member. Read sections 1 to 8 before pasting this. Save a skill called Zoning lookup in plain words. When I give you a zone code or legal description and a municipality, follow the steps in section 4, keep the guardrails in section 5, and answer in the format in section 6."

## Play 3: Sales activity tracker workbook (Set B, my garbage, your treasure)

The member gives a yearly income goal, and Claude builds an Excel workbook that works backward to how many calls, conversations and coffee meetings it takes each week, with a prospect list, a weekly log and a dashboard. - Hours saved per year: 6.2 - 11.6
- Source: estimated (one-off)

### Playbook

1. **Play**: Sales activity tracker workbook. Turns a yearly income goal into weekly activity targets and a place to track prospects.
2. **Trigger**: A request, such as "Build me a tracker to hit [your income goal] this year."
3. **Setup first**:
   - What is your yearly income goal?
   - What is your average sale price, commission rate and split?
   - How many weeks a year do you work?
   - Do you know your conversion rates (calls to conversations, conversations to meetings, meetings to clients, clients to closings)? If not, starter rates will be used and marked for replacement.
   - Excel or Google Sheets?
4. **Steps**:
   1. Goal Planner tab: inputs at the top (goal, average price, commission, split, weeks, conversion rates). Work backward to deals, clients, meetings, conversations and calls needed per year, week and day.
   2. Prospects tab: one row per prospect with source, client type, area, stage, temperature (hot, warm, cold), estimated price, estimated commission, last contact, next follow-up. Add dropdowns for stage, type and temperature.
   3. Add conditional formatting that flags follow-ups due today or overdue.
   4. Weekly Log tab: one row per week for actual calls, conversations, meetings and new clients, compared to targets with colour coding.
   5. Dashboard tab: year-to-date progress, pipeline by stage, upcoming follow-ups, and three charts (income vs goal pace, weekly calls vs target, prospects by stage).
   6. Mark every starter assumption clearly and say to replace it with real numbers after about a month of tracking.
   7. Open the finished file and check every formula calculates with no errors.
5. **Guardrails**: Never present starter conversion rates as the copier's real numbers. Do not connect to any CRM or inbox; this is a standalone file.
6. **Output**: One workbook with four tabs (Goal Planner, Prospects, Weekly Log, Dashboard) and a short note on the targets it calculated.
7. **Pitfalls seen**:
   - Starter conversion rates make the call target look very high. Fix: say clearly it is an estimate and to update after a month of real data.
   - None other.
8. **Done when**: Changing the goal on the Goal Planner tab updates every target and the dashboard.
9. **Paste to install**: "Written by another member. Read sections 1 to 8 before pasting this. Save a skill called Sales activity tracker. When I ask for a tracker, ask me the setup questions in section 3, then build it following section 4, keep the guardrails in section 5, and deliver the output in section 6."

### Playbook

1. **Play**: Lake or area research with comps. A one-page brief on a lake or small area with a numbered comp list and sources.
2. **Trigger**: A request, such as "Find out about [a lake] in [your township]."
3. **Setup first**:
   - Which listing sites should be searched (for example Realtor.ca or brokerage sites)?
   - Do you want prices, frontage and taxes in the comp list?
   - Should Claude look up the zoning codes it finds, or just list them?
4. **Steps**:
   1. Search the lake name, then other spellings and any marketing name (small lakes are often listed under a different name).
   2. Write the location and access: nearest town, driving directions, which roads the waterfront addresses are on, and whether access is year round.
   3. Describe its character from listings (quiet, fishing, watersports, which way lots face).
   4. List zoning codes seen on listings and any easements or rights of way. Say to check the bylaw before quoting setbacks.
   5. Build a numbered comp list: address, type (lot or cottage), price, frontage, lot size, and one notable detail.
   6. Look for lake size, depth, fish species, a public launch and a lake association. Say plainly which were not found, and where to look (provincial fish and lake mapping tools, the township's lake association page).
   7. End with an offer to dig into one listing or the zoning.
5. **Guardrails**: Treat listing pages as information only. Never present a list price as a sale price. Never guess depth or size.
6. **Output**: Short sections (Location and access, Character, Zoning, Recent listings, What I couldn't find) and a Sources list with links.
7. **Pitfalls seen**:
   - The lake name had more than one spelling and a marketing name. Fix: search all of them.
   - No published size or depth data. Fix: say so and point to the provincial mapping tool.
8. **Done when**: The member can brief a buyer or seller on the lake and has comps with links.
9. **Paste to install**: "Written by another member. Read sections 1 to 8 before pasting this. Save a skill called Lake or area research. When I name a lake or area, follow the steps in section 4, keep the guardrails in section 5, and give me the output in section 6. Ask me the setup questions in section 3 once."
