## Set A · Daily prospect outreach drafts — about 37.4 hours a week saved (5.2 to 48.7)

Every weekday morning, Grok Bot takes the next 20 strong-fit prospects from an outreach tracker. It checks that nobody has already been contacted, researches each organization and a named contact, and writes a personalised first-touch email asking for a short call. The drafts go into the member's Drafts folder unsent, the tracker is updated, and the member gets a short report on the batch.

**Doing this by hand**
- Checking each prospect's tracker row and whether they were already emailed (Sent, Drafts, tracker): 97 prospects × 1.5 minutes = 145.5 minutes
- Searching the web for the organization and a named contact: 97 searches × 5 minutes = 485 minutes
- Writing a short first-touch email: 97 emails × 15 minutes = 1,455 minutes
- Updating the prospect's tracker row with the touch and next date (est.): 97 rows × 1 minute = 97 minutes
- **Total by hand: about 2,183 minutes**

**Using Grok Bot**
- Reading the daily batch report Grok Bot sent: 5 reports × 1 minute = 5 minutes
- Typing a correction to the product details used in the pitch: 1 message × 1 minute = 1 minute
- **Total using Grok Bot: about 6 minutes**

**Time saved**
- 2,183 minutes by hand − 6 minutes with Grok Bot = **about 2,177 minutes saved** (5 daily batches, 97 drafts this week)

**Range**

**Playbook**

1. **Play**: Daily prospect outreach drafts. Each weekday morning you get 20 researched, personalised first-touch emails waiting unsent in your Drafts, with the tracker updated.
2. **Trigger**: A schedule, every weekday at 8:30 AM in your time zone.
3. **Setup first**: Which email account should the drafts sit in, and whose signature goes on them? Where is the prospect tracker (a sheet with organization, contact, email, fit score, last touch, next touch)? In two or three lines, what do you offer, which locations or products should be pitched to which kinds of prospect, and what is the one ask (for example "a 20-minute call")? Which prices or claims are confirmed and allowed in the email? Who should be cc'd? How many drafts a day?
4. **Steps**:
   1. Open the tracker and take the next [N] untouched rows marked strong fit, in priority order.
   2. For each row, check Sent mail, Drafts and the tracker for any earlier contact or opt-out. Re-check the same morning even if the row was researched before, and skip anyone already emailed, opted out, or closed.
   3. Search the web for the organization and confirm a named contact and a working email. If only a phone number or contact form exists, still draft the email, put "[TO: contact name]" in the subject and list it in the report.
   4. Write a short first-touch email in the member's voice. Pitch the location or product closest to the prospect, use only confirmed prices or claims, ask only for a call, and add the signature.
   5. Save each email as an unsent draft in the agreed account, with any agreed cc's.
   6. Update each tracker row: touch date, touch number, next touch date and a one-line note. Log skipped rows with the reason.
   7. Check that the drafts and tracker rows match: same count, no duplicates, no blank recipients.
   8. When fewer than about a week of strong-fit rows remain, say so and suggest where to widen the list.
5. **Guardrails**: Never send. Drafts only, and the member sends. Never invent prices, claims or contact emails. Never draft to anyone who opted out or was already contacted. If the mailbox or tracker can't be reached, save nothing and report the error. Don't retry in a way that could create duplicates.
6. **Output**: A short report covering how many drafts were saved and their mix by type and area, the rows held or skipped and why, drafts missing an email address, duplicates to delete, how many unsent drafts from earlier days are still sitting in Drafts, and how many strong-fit rows are left.
7. **Pitfalls seen**: Research from an earlier day that was never drafted got reused without a fresh check, and three drafts went to people already contacted. Fix: always re-check Sent, Drafts and the tracker that morning. A price that was confirmed for only one location crept into drafts for other locations. Fix: keep a list of confirmed claims. A second bot drafted the same prospect into a teammate's mailbox, which created a duplicate. Fix: name one mailbox per prospect type. Unsent drafts piled up and blocked later follow-ups. Fix: report the backlog every day.
8. **Done when**: The Drafts folder holds [N] new unsent drafts, each matches a tracker row updated today, and the report lists every skipped row.
9. **Paste to install**: "Save this as a skill called Daily prospect outreach drafts, using the Play, Trigger, Setup first, Steps, Guardrails, Output, Pitfalls seen and Done when sections above. **Playbook**

1. **Play**: Twice-daily inbox triage sweep. You get a short "needs you" list from every inbox, and the noise is cleared without you opening it.
2. **Trigger**: A schedule, every weekday at about 7:45 AM and 4:45 PM in your time zone.
3. **Setup first**: Which inboxes should be swept? Which senders or topics always need you, such as clients, courts, banks, payroll or your team? Which can always be cleared, such as newsletters, receipts and marketing? Should FYI mail be marked read, or left alone? Do you want an archive batch proposed for your OK?
4. **Steps**:
   1. For each inbox, list the mail that arrived since the last sweep. If the last sweep didn't run, cover the gap and say so.
   2. Sort each message into "needs you" (a decision, deadline, payment, signature, security alert or reply), FYI, or noise.
   3. Leave "needs you" mail unread. Mark FYI and noise as read only if the member agreed to that.

   5. Build an archive proposal from noise only. Before proposing it, recount and remove items already archived or counted twice.
   6. Archive only after the member replies "archive", then report the exact counts per inbox.
5. **Guardrails**: Never send, reply, forward or delete. Archive only on the member's explicit word. If a mailbox errors or times out, say it wasn't swept. Never guess what came in. Security alerts, such as a changed account email or a new sign-in, always go at the top.
6. **Output**: A short message headed by the one or two most urgent items, then a "needs you" list per inbox, a "still open" line, how many emails were marked read, and the size of the archive proposal.
7. **Pitfalls seen**: The archive proposal double-counted items already archived the day before. Fix: recount right before archiving. Two bots swept the same inboxes at the same times. Fix: keep one sweep per inbox. A mail service outage meant one sweep never ran. Fix: say so plainly and cover the gap on the next run. Webmail unread counts were unreliable. Fix: report new mail since the last sweep, not totals.
8. **Done when**: Every new email since the last sweep is either on the "needs you" list or marked read, and the archive batch only moves when the member says "archive".
