## 1. Turn long talks into ready draft reels: about 8.0 hours a week saved (5.5 to 10.5)

Grok Bot goes through a long recorded class or talk, finds the strongest story moment, and cuts it into a vertical draft reel with a type-only cover and a caption that credits the speaker. It starts when the member sends a link or asks for today's class, or when a posting-day routine asks for a draft. The member gets a short list of moments with timestamps, then a ready-to-review reel, and nothing is posted without a yes.

**Doing this by hand**
- Finding the class in the archive or opening the link and getting the full talk: 1 search × 5 minutes = 5 minutes (Default)
- Skimming a 30 to 90 minute class to find the best story moment: 1 class × 30 minutes = 30 minutes (est.)
- Cutting the clip on a clean first beat and removing pauses and false starts: 1 cut × 20 minutes = 20 minutes (est.)
- Making a vertical type-only cover with the hook line: 1 cover × 10 minutes = 10 minutes (est.)
- Writing the caption with speaker and source credit: 1 caption × 15 minutes = 15 minutes (Default)
- Exporting and uploading the full-quality file to Drive: 1 upload × 3 minutes = 3 minutes (est.)
- **Total by hand: 83 minutes**

- Picking a moment from the short list: 3 picks × 0.25 minutes = 0.75 minutes
- Reading the short lists and watching the draft reels Grok Bot sent: 12 reads × 1 minute = 12 minutes
- **Total using Grok Bot: about 30 minutes across 9 reels, about 3.3 minutes a reel**

### Playbook

**1. Play**
Turn long talks into ready draft reels. One long class or talk becomes a 60 to 120 second vertical draft reel with a type cover and a credited caption, ready to approve.

**2. Trigger**
A request in chat ("any good clip here [link]", "what's a good clip in today's class"), or a posting-day routine (for example Sunday, Tuesday and Thursday at 9:00 AM in your time zone) that asks for one ready draft or a one-line "well empty, hunting [topic]".

**3. Setup first**
- Where do your source talks live (a class archive, a channel, links you send)? Is there a freshness rule, such as only talks from the past week?
- Which account do the reels go to, and what is its lane (language, topics, story-first or teaching)?
- Which sources must never be used (other pages' edited shorts)?
- Cover style: colors, fonts, face or no face?
- Target length, and is a small speed-up (up to 1.3x) acceptable?
- Credit format: speaker name, source class, handles to tag, and whether speakers are tagged only or also invited as collaborators.
- Quiet hours when nothing should be sent or posted (for example [day] after [time] through [day]).
- Where full-quality files go (a Google Drive folder).
- Connect Google Drive. Save the posting account's login in the vault only if you want Grok Bot to post.

**4. Steps**
1. Find the source: the newest class in the archive, or the link sent. If today's class is not up yet, say when it usually appears and check again then.
2. Get a full-quality copy. If a download needs a sign-in, use the saved login or hand the screen over once. If your own computer is offline, use Grok Bot's own browser session instead of waiting.
3. Transcribe with timestamps and list the 2 or 3 best story moments, each with start and end times and a one-line hook. Recommend one.
4. If the class has little story, say so and offer the one real moment even if it is short. Do not pad it with technical passages.
5. Cut from the full talk. Open on a clear first beat, never mid-story. End on a clean closing line. Remove ums, false starts and long pauses. Aim for 60 to 120 seconds; speed up to at most 1.3x only if needed.
6. Crop to 9:16 and check still frames from the start, middle and end so the speaker stays in frame and no text is cut off.
7. Make a type-only cover in the account's style with the hook line.
8. Write a story-first caption: the hook, two or three lines of the story, a credit line for the speaker and the source class, verified handles, and hashtags.
9. Deliver a small preview in chat plus the full-quality file in Drive, with the length and the source timestamps.
10. If the member says post, check the active account, post once, apply tags and collaborator invites exactly as set up, then send the live link.

**5. Guardrails**
- Draft only. Never post, schedule or invite collaborators without an explicit yes for that piece.
- Always recut from the full talk and credit the speaker. Never reuse another page's edited short.
- Nothing goes out during the set quiet hours.
- Stop after one failed login so the account does not lock, and ask the member to reset or update the saved login.
- If a download or site is blocked, say what is blocked and offer the screen hand-off. Never work around a sign-in.

**6. Output**
First, a short list: 2 or 3 moments with timestamps, a one-line hook each, and a pick. Then the draft: reel preview in chat, full-quality Drive file, cover, caption text, length and the source timestamps, marked "draft, not posted".

**7. Pitfalls seen**
- A first cut started in the middle of the story. Fix: always open on the true first beat.
- A reel showed as "unavailable" in chat because the file was too large. Fix: send a compressed preview and put the full file in Drive.
- The speaker was cropped out of frame on a two-panel layout. Fix: check stills before delivering.
- Downloads stalled while the member's computer was offline. Fix: use Grok Bot's own signed-in browser session.
- The saved posting login was out of date after a password reset. Fix: update the vault entry right after any reset.
- A speaker got a collaborator invite when only a tag was wanted. Fix: set the tag versus collaborator rule at setup and follow it.

**8. Done when**
The member has a playable draft reel, cover and caption that open on a clear first beat, and either approves it, asks for a recut, or it goes up with the right tags.

**9. Paste to install**
"Save this as a skill called 'Long talk to draft reel', following the Play, Trigger, Setup first, Steps, Guardrails, Output, Pitfalls seen and Done when sections exactly. Ask me the Setup first questions once and remember my answers. ### Playbook

**1. Play**
Weekday news scout with ready post drafts. Each weekday morning you get 2 or 3 sourced post drafts in your voice about what moved in your niche overnight.

**2. Trigger**
A schedule: weekdays at 7:00 AM in your time zone (it may start up to 15 minutes early). Also on request: "run the scout again now".

**3. Setup first**
- What is your niche, in a few topics?
- Which news sources and which accounts on X and LinkedIn should it watch?
- What view count makes a post "taking off" (for example 5,000)?
- Paste 3 to 5 of your best posts so it learns your voice. What should it never do (example lists, questions at the end, invented examples)?
- How many drafts a day, and should it stay silent on quiet days?
- Who posts approved drafts (you, or a teammate through your task list)?
- Connect X if you want it to read posts directly; LinkedIn is read through the browser after you sign in once.

**4. Steps**
1. Search the news for your niche's stories from the last 24 hours.
2. Search X (and LinkedIn if signed in) for niche posts above the view threshold.
3. Open each candidate's original source and check the facts. When a trending summary disagrees with the original, use the original and say what was corrected.
4. Drop anything you already posted about or that overlaps a recent post.
5. Pick 2 or 3 by timing and relevance, and say which is the strongest.
6. Write each in your voice. For news and rule changes use the short form: a plain headline opener, what happened, why it matters to your audience, what changed today, and end on a prediction instead of a question. Keep it to about 60 to 80 words. Label any made-up example as an example. Use the longer, number-heavy form only for data studies.
7. Under each draft add the source link and one line on why it is moving.
8. End with one line listing what was seen but skipped, so you can ask about any of them.
9. When you rewrite a draft yourself, save your version into the voice skill as the new example.

**5. Guardrails**
- Drafts only. Never post, schedule or reply anywhere without an explicit yes.
- No client names or details in any draft.
- Every fact needs a source link. If something cannot be verified, leave it out or say so.
- If draft cards are not available, paste the copy in chat; never post to work around it.

**6. Output**
A short lead line ("Three drafts for today, nothing posted"), then each draft with its source link and "why it's moving", the pick, and a one-line skipped list.

**7. Pitfalls seen**
- X was quiet in the niche on most days. Fix: fall back to news and say so in the lead line.
- A trending summary had the wrong date range for a key statistic. Fix: always check the original source.
- An earlier draft misdescribed where a quote came from. Fix: read the transcript, not the coverage, before quoting.
- Drafts came out longer and more explanatory than the member writes. Fix: the short news form in step 6, with the member's own post saved as the example.

**8. Done when**
On weekday mornings you have 2 or 3 sourced drafts you could post with light edits, and on quiet days you hear nothing.

### Playbook

**1. Play**
Market-close recap as a motion video. A 30 to 45 second vertical video of the day's close, built from verified closing numbers, ready to post.

**2. Trigger**
A request after the close ("make a motion graphic video of today's market results"). For daily use, a schedule on trading days at 4:30 PM ET.

**3. Setup first**

- Style: colors, fonts, logo, music or silent?
- What disclaimer line do you want (for example "Not investment advice")?

**4. Steps**
1. Wait for the official close. Use closing figures, never intraday ones.
2. Pull each number from two sources and confirm the date. Drop anything you cannot verify, and flag anything confirmed by only one source.
3. Save the numbers with their sources to a data file before building anything.
4. Build the video from code with a code-based video renderer, not AI video: a title card with the date and the headline, the indexes counting up, rates, oil and crypto, top gainers and losers as bars, your watchlist, a one-line takeaway, a sources line and the disclaimer.
5. Export 2 or 3 still frames and check that text is legible, nothing overlaps or clips, and every number matches the data file. Fix and re-render.
6. Deliver the MP4 with a table of every number shown and its sources, plus what was dropped or not re-verified.

**5. Guardrails**
- Real data only, never placeholders.
- No buy or sell advice, and always the disclaimer.
- Do not post anywhere without an explicit yes.
- Label any after-hours or earlier figure that was not re-checked.

**6. Output**
A vertical MP4 (30 to 45 seconds, 30 fps) and a table with columns Item | Close | Change | Sources, followed by a short caveats list.

**7. Pitfalls seen**

- A watchlist figure in the brief was an intraday price; the close was different. Fix: always re-pull closes.
- One index was confirmed by only one source. Fix: flag it in the caveats.

### Playbook

**1. Play**
Turn a process call into workflow Pages. One call becomes a detailed current versus proposed workflow map plus a visual leadership one-pager that makes the case and the ask.

**2. Trigger**
A request with a call link: "map the workflow from this call", optionally with a screenshot of today's process. Follow-up: "make a version for leadership".

**3. Setup first**
- Which call recorder do you use? Connect it so Grok Bot can read transcripts and summaries.
- Who is the audience for the map (the team who fills in details) and for the one-pager (the decision makers)?
- What decision or approval is the goal (a tool, a budget, a rollout)?
- Any style for Pages (brand colors, logo)?
- Should Grok Bot offer to draft the cover note to the team (never send it)?

**4. Steps**
1. Pull the transcript and the summary from the call recorder. Read the process screenshot if one was sent; if the file cannot be opened, copy its steps into the brief as text.
2. Find the member's own action item from the call and build toward it.
3. Page 1, the detailed map: the current flow step by step, each break point, the proposed flow, any interim workaround, a phased rollout, the approvals needed, and open questions for named roles. Mark anything not said on the call as "to be confirmed".
4. Flag conflicts: where the screenshot and the transcript disagree, who owns a manual step if nobody said, likely misheard names, and who really makes the final decision.
5. Page 2, the leadership one-pager: the stakes in one line, before and after side by side with numbered break points, one item's journey through each flow, count tiles using only countable facts from the call (systems, hand-offs, delays), a three-phase rollout, the ask, a risks table, and a link to Page 1.
6. Send both links with 3 to 5 points to check before sharing, and offer to draft the team note.

**5. Guardrails**
- No invented numbers, names or decisions. Unknowns say "to be measured" or "to be confirmed".
- Never share the Pages or message anyone without an explicit yes.
- Keep people's private details and anything sensitive off the leadership page.

**6. Output**
Two Page links: the detailed workflow map and the visual leadership one-pager, plus a short list of points to confirm (owners, decision makers, assumptions marked as proposed).

**7. Pitfalls seen**
- The process screenshot path could not be opened by the worker building the Page. Fix: paste the screenshot's steps into the brief as text.
- Nobody on the call named who does a key manual upload. Fix: mark it "to be confirmed" and list it.
- The decision maker for one system was not who was assumed. Fix: state the assumption on the Page.
- The transcript misheard a name. Fix: flag likely mishearings instead of repeating them.
- The leadership Page was never checked on a big screen. Fix: tell the member to preview it on the presenting screen.

**8. Done when**
The team can fill in the details on Page 1, and the decision makers can understand the case and the ask from Page 2 in under a minute.
